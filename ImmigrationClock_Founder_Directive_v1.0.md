# ImmigrationClock Founder Directive (Part 2)

## The Problem We Exist to Solve

The United States immigration system is one of the most complex public systems in the world. Information exists across dozens of agencies, press releases, datasets, court decisions, policy memoranda, and news stories.

People are forced to assemble their understanding from scattered sources.

ImmigrationClock exists to organize that information into a coherent, trustworthy knowledge system.

We are not trying to publish the most content.

We are trying to produce the clearest understanding.

---

## Our Audience

We build for:

- Individuals navigating immigration.
- Families supporting loved ones.
- International students.
- Employers sponsoring talent.
- HR and global mobility teams.
- Immigration attorneys.
- Journalists.
- Researchers.
- Policymakers.
- Anyone who wants factual immigration information.

Every feature should clearly identify which audience it primarily serves.

---

## The Four Pillars

### 1. Official Sources First

Whenever possible, information should originate from official government sources.

Examples include:

- USCIS
- Department of State
- DHS
- CBP
- ICE
- Department of Labor
- Federal Register
- Executive Orders
- Federal Courts
- Congress

Secondary reporting may provide context, but official records remain the source of truth whenever available.

---

### 2. Explain, Don't Amplify

ImmigrationClock should not compete with newspapers on speed.

Instead, it should explain:

- what happened,
- why it matters,
- who is affected,
- how it compares historically,
- and where the information originated.

---

### 3. Connect Information

Every important object should connect to related information.

Examples:

Employer → WARN Notices → H-1B History → Related News → Government Sources

Country → Embassy → Visa Information → Policy Changes → Travel Guidance

Visa Category → Official Guidance → Processing Trends → FAQs → Historical Timeline

The platform is a knowledge graph, not a collection of isolated pages.

---

### 4. Earn Daily Trust

Trust is earned through consistency.

Every page should display, where appropriate:

- Original source
- Last updated date
- Data-through date
- Methodology
- Confidence or classification
- Known limitations

Users should never have to wonder where information came from.

---

## Product Evolution Principles

Every new feature should satisfy at least one of these goals:

- Improve understanding.
- Improve discoverability.
- Improve transparency.
- Improve historical context.
- Improve decision making.
- Improve trust.

Avoid features that exist only because competitors have them.

---

## Product Decision Test

Before approving a feature, answer these questions:

1. Does this help someone understand immigration better?
2. Does this strengthen trust?
3. Does this connect users with official information?
4. Is it likely to remain valuable five years from now?
5. Would we proudly explain this feature to a journalist or researcher?

If the answer to most questions is no, reconsider the feature.

---

## Closing Principle

ImmigrationClock should become known for one thing above all else:

When people need to understand U.S. immigration, they know they can rely on ImmigrationClock for clear, sourced, and connected information.

# ImmigrationClock Founder Directive (Part 4)

# Technical Execution Philosophy

Your responsibility is not to maximize feature count.

Your responsibility is to maximize long-term trust, maintainability, and adaptability.

Every engineering decision should support the 10-year vision.

---

# Event-Driven Architecture

ImmigrationClock revolves around structured immigration events.

An event may originate from:

- USCIS
- Department of State
- DHS
- CBP
- ICE
- Department of Labor
- Federal Register
- Executive Orders
- Federal Courts
- Congress
- Verified government datasets

Each event should include:

- Stable identifier
- Source agency
- Published date
- Effective date (if different)
- Data-through date (if applicable)
- Classification
- Severity
- Summary
- Related entities
- Original source URL
- Last verification timestamp

Events become the foundation for pages, timelines, alerts, newsletters, APIs, and AI.

---

# AI Boundaries

AI assists.

Official data decides.

AI may:

- summarize
- explain
- compare
- organize
- answer questions using indexed ImmigrationClock knowledge

AI must never:

- invent policy
- fabricate statistics
- hide uncertainty
- replace official citations
- create unsupported legal conclusions

Every AI answer should reference the underlying source whenever possible.

---

# Repository Principles

Organize the repository around domains rather than isolated pages.

Examples:

- events
- employers
- visas
- countries
- agencies
- datasets
- search
- alerts
- intelligence
- shared UI
- shared services

Avoid duplicated business logic.

---

# Engineering Standards

Prefer:

- modular services
- reusable components
- strongly typed models
- automated testing
- documented interfaces
- predictable naming
- clear separation of concerns

Optimize for readability over cleverness.

Assume another engineer or AI agent will maintain this code years from now.

---

# Security

Treat public trust as a security feature.

Protect:

- credentials
- user information
- API keys
- administrative actions

Never expose secrets in the repository.

Never assume external data is trustworthy without validation.

---

# Data Freshness

Every major page should communicate:

- Source
- Last refreshed
- Data-through date
- Published date
- Methodology

Users should always know how current the information is.

---

# Analytics

Measure:

- What users search for
- Which questions remain unanswered
- Which pages build trust
- Which features increase return visits

Do not optimize only for clicks.

Optimize for successful understanding.

---

# Engineering Decision Framework

When choosing between two technical solutions ask:

1. Which option preserves trust?
2. Which option is easier to maintain?
3. Which option scales to millions of users?
4. Which option supports future knowledge graph growth?
5. Which option makes future AI assistance easier?

Document tradeoffs whenever the answer is not obvious.

---

# Final Principle

Every pull request should leave ImmigrationClock more trustworthy than it was before.
