ACTION: Notice of proposed rulemaking. ----------------------------------------------------------------------- SUMMARY: In the Notice section of today's Federal Register, the Executive Office for Immigration Review (EOIR), a component within the United States Department of Justice (DOJ or Department), has published a notice of a new system of records titled ``Fraud and Abuse Prevention Program Records'' and numbered JUSTICE/EOIR-006. This system of records is exempt from U.S.C. 552a(c)(3) and (d) pursuant to 5 U.S.C. 552a (k)(2). EOIR proposes to exempt this system of records from certain provisions of the Privacy Act in order to protect law enforcement sensitive materials maintained in the system. For the reasons provided below, the Department proposes to amend its Privacy Act regulations by exempting records in this system from certain provisions of the Privacy Act. Public comment is invited. SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: I. Background The primary mission of EOIR is to adjudicate immigration cases by fairly, expeditiously, and uniformly interpreting and administering the Nation's immigration laws. Under delegated authority from the Attorney General, EOIR conducts immigration court proceedings, appellate reviews, and administrative hearings. Individuals who are subjects of EOIR immigration proceedings may be represented by counsel. Within EOIR's Office of the General Counsel (OGC), under the supervision of a designated anti-fraud officer, the Fraud and Abuse Prevention Program (Fraud Program) investigates complaints of immigration fraud, immigration scams, and the unauthorized practice of immigration law within EOIR proceedings. The Fraud Program refers complaints of fraud to appropriate law enforcement and disciplinary authorities; and it provides training for EOIR personnel, outreach materials, and education for the public on these issues. See 8 CFR 1003.0(f)(2). The anti-fraud officer serves as a point of contact relating to concerns about possible fraud involving EOIR proceedings, particularly with respect to matters relating to fraudulent applications or documents affecting multiple removal proceedings, applications for relief from removal, appeals, or other proceedings before EOIR. 8 CFR 1003.0(f)(2)(i). The Fraud Program coordinates with investigative authorities of the Department of Justice, Department of Homeland Security, and other appropriate agencies with respect to the identification of and response to reported fraud. 8 CFR 1003.0(f)(2)(ii). The Fraud Program also collaborates with the EOIR disciplinary counsel and with other appropriate authorities with respect to instances of fraud, misrepresentation, or abuse pertaining to an attorney or accredited representative providing representation to clients in EOIR immigration proceedings. 8 CFR 1003.0(f)(2)(iii). The Fraud Program maintains a system of records in connection with its work receiving, tracking, managing, investigating, and responding to reports of suspected immigration fraud, abuse, and unauthorized practice of immigration law. The Fraud Program refers complaints to appropriate Federal, state, local, territorial, or tribal law enforcement and disciplinary authorities for further investigation into potential violations of civil and/or criminal law. When referring complaints, the Fraud Program will provide authorities with copies of records maintained in the system. The records include information pertaining to complainants, witnesses, and subjects of investigations of alleged fraud and individuals (EOIR adjudicators or personnel, or members of the public) reporting or observing alleged fraud. Such records contain law enforcement sensitive information and are properly exempt from various provisions of the Privacy Act pursuant to 5 U.S.C. 552a(k)(2). II. Privacy Act Exemptions The Privacy Act allows Federal agencies to exempt eligible records in a system of records from certain provisions of the Act, including those that provide individuals with a right to request access to and amendment of records about the individual. If an agency intends to exempt a particular system of records, it must first issue a rulemaking pursuant to 5 U.S.C. 553(b)(1)-(3), (c), and (e). The Department proposes to modify 28 CFR part 16 to amend the Privacy Act exemptions for the new system of records, Fraud and Abuse Prevention Program Records, JUSTICE/EOIR-006. Specifically, in section 16.83, the Department proposes to add paragraph (g) claiming certain records within the new system of records are exempt from 5 U.S.C. 552a(c)(3) and (d) and (2) pursuant to 5 U.S.C. 552a(k)(2). The exemption is justified because access to or an accounting for disclosures of records contained in this system of records could inform the subject of the investigation of an actual or potential criminal, civil, or regulatory violation or the existence of that investigation; of the nature and scope of the information and evidence obtained by the investigation as to the subject's activities; of the identity of confidential sources, witnesses, and law enforcement personnel; and of information that may enable the subject to avoid detection or apprehension. These factors would present a serious impediment to effective law and regulatory enforcement where they prevent the successful completion of the investigation; endanger the physical safety of confidential sources, witnesses, and law enforcement personnel; and/or lead to the improper influencing of witnesses, the destruction of evidence, or the fabrication of testimony. These exemptions apply only to the extent that information in this system of records is subject to exemption pursuant to 5 U.S.C. 552a (k)(2). To the extent that information in a record pertaining to an individual does not relate to official Federal investigations and/or law enforcement matters, the exemption does not apply. In addition, where compliance would not appear to interfere with or adversely affect the overall law or regulatory enforcement process, the applicable exemption may be waived by EOIR.
