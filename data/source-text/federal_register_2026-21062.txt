ACTION: Notice of a new system of records. ----------------------------------------------------------------------- SUMMARY: Pursuant to the Privacy Act of 1974 and Office of Management and Budget (OMB) Circular No. A-108, notice is hereby given that the Executive Office for Immigration Review (EOIR), a component within the United States Department of Justice (DOJ or Department), proposes to develop a new system of records notice titled ``Fraud and Abuse Prevention Program Records,'' and numbered JUSTICE/EOIR-006. This system of records assists the EOIR Fraud and Abuse Prevention Program (Fraud Program) in receiving, tracking, and managing complaints of suspected immigration fraud, abuse, and unauthorized practice of immigration law, and in referring complaints to Federal, state, and local law enforcement and disciplinary authorities when appropriate. Fraud Program records were previously included within a different system of records, JUSTICE/EOIR-001. See 69 FR 16179 (May 11, 2004). However, JUSTICE/EOIR-001 was recently modified and now only partially covers Fraud Program records. See JUSTICE/EOIR-001, Adjudication and Appeal Records of the Office of the Chief Immigration Judge and Board of Immigration Appeals, 90 FR 42265 (Aug. 29, 2025). Accordingly, EOIR proposes to establish a new system of records encompassing all Fraud Program records. DATES: In accordance with 5 U.S.C. 552a(e)(4) and (11), this notice is effective upon publication, subject to a 30-day period in which to comment on the routine uses, described below. Routine uses will become effective after the 30-day comment period. Please submit any comments by November 16, 2026. SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: The primary mission of EOIR is to adjudicate immigration cases by fairly, expeditiously, and uniformly interpreting and administering the Nation's immigration laws. Under delegated authority from the Attorney General, EOIR conducts immigration court proceedings, appellate reviews, and administrative hearings. Individuals and aliens who are subjects of EOIR immigration proceedings may be represented by counsel. Within EOIR's Office of the General Counsel (OGC), under the supervision of a designated anti-fraud officer, the Fraud and Abuse Prevention Program (Fraud Program) investigates complaints of immigration fraud, immigration scams, and the unauthorized practice of immigration law within EOIR proceedings. The Fraud Program refers complaints of fraud to appropriate law enforcement and disciplinary authorities; and it provides training for EOIR personnel, outreach materials, and education for the public on these issues. See 8 CFR 1003.0(f)(2). The anti-fraud officer serves as a point of contact relating to concerns about possible fraud involving EOIR proceedings, particularly with respect to matters relating to fraudulent applications or documents affecting multiple removal proceedings, applications for relief from removal, appeals, or other proceedings before EOIR. 8 CFR 1003.0(f)(2)(i). The Fraud Program coordinates with investigative authorities of the Department of Justice, Department of Homeland Security, and other appropriate agencies with respect to the identification of and response to reported fraud. 8 CFR 1003.0(f)(2)(ii). The Fraud Program also collaborates with the EOIR disciplinary counsel and with other appropriate authorities with [[Page 65603]] respect to instances of fraud, misrepresentation, or abuse pertaining to an attorney or accredited representative providing representation to clients in EOIR immigration proceedings. 8 CFR 1003.0(f)(2)(iii). This system of records is used by the Fraud Program in connection with its work receiving, tracking, managing, investigating, and responding to reports of suspected immigration fraud, abuse, and unauthorized practice of immigration law, and referring complaints to Federal, state, local, territorial, or tribal law enforcement and disciplinary authorities when appropriate. The system consists primarily of electronic records. Records received in hard copy are digitized and the hard copies are disposed of following quality control checks of the digital copies, in accordance with authorized agency records information management practices. Records are stored on the DOJ EOIR Microsoft Azure Cloud in agency shared drives and disposed of in accordance with applicable record retention schedules. As such, EOIR has prepared this system of records notice to account for the changes to JUSTICE/EOIR-001, resulting in the need for a system of records specific to the Fraud Program system of records. Finally, pursuant to 5 U.S.C. 552a(k)(2), EOIR claims that information in this system is exempt from 5 U.S.C. 552a(c) and (d). Rules will be promulgated in accordance with the requirements of 5 U.S.C. 553(b), (c), and (e) and will be published and codified in the Federal Register at 28 CFR 16.83. In accordance with 5 U.S.C. 552a(r), the Department has provided a report to OMB and Congress on this new system of records. Dated: October 5, 2026. Laurence E. PURPOSE(S) OF THE SYSTEM: The purpose of this system is to maintain records of the Fraud Program in connection with its work receiving, tracking, managing, investigating, and responding to reports of suspected immigration fraud, abuse, and unauthorized practice of immigration law, and referring complaints to law enforcement and disciplinary authorities when appropriate. The records in the system are also used to generate statistical reports, informational materials for public awareness concerning immigration fraud issues, and internal materials to train EOIR personnel in identifying and reporting suspected fraud. CATEGORIES OF INDIVIDUALS COVERED BY THE SYSTEM: This system includes records pertaining to complainants, witnesses, and subjects of investigations of alleged fraud; EOIR adjudicators and personnel reporting, observing, or investigating alleged fraud; individuals who are subjects of EOIR immigration proceedings; attorneys, representatives, and organizations practicing before EOIR adjudicators; and law enforcement and disciplinary authorities to which the Fraud Program refers complaints. CATEGORIES OF RECORDS IN THE SYSTEM: The system includes the following categories of electronic records: Fraud Program case files, related data, and education and training materials. Fraud Program case files consist of: complaints of fraud, including the complainant's contact information; evidentiary records submitted with complaints or collected during the course of investigating and substantiating complaints, including copies of relevant portions of official records of immigration proceedings and digital audio recordings of immigration hearings; correspondence, witnesses statements, memoranda, and notes collected or created in the course of investigating and substantiating complaints; correspondence with law enforcement and disciplinary authorities to which a complaint may be referred. Fraud Program data contained in the records in this system consists of: complaints, including case number and status; the date a complaint or case was reported or closed; names and contact information of complainants, witnesses, subjects, and law enforcement or disciplinary authorities; and date of birth, age, sex, and physical descriptors of the complainants, witnesses, or subjects, as well as their alien registration number (A-number), and/or EOIR ID numbers. Fraud Program data is used to generate statistical reports. This system includes general educational materials, including the Fraud Program Newsletter, created by the Fraud Program for purposes of informing and raising internal and/or public awareness concerning immigration-related fraud issues. The system also includes internal training materials and presentations developed by the Fraud Program to train EOIR personnel on identifying and reporting suspected fraud. RECORD SOURCE CATEGORIES: Records in this system are generated or provided by members of the public; EOIR personnel; and law enforcement or disciplinary authorities. ROUTINE USES OF RECORDS MAINTAINED IN THE SYSTEM, INCLUDING CATEGORIES OF USERS AND THE PURPOSES OF SUCH USES: In addition to those disclosures generally permitted under 5 U.S.C. 552a(b), all or a portion of the records contained in this system of records may be disclosed as a routine use pursuant to 5 U.S.C. 552a(b)(3) under the circumstances or for the purposes described below, to the extent such disclosures are compatible with the purposes for which the information in the record was collected: (1) To the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), including but not [[Page 65604]] limited to the Disciplinary Counsel(s) for the U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), Homeland Security Investigations, and Fraud Detection and National Security Directorate, to investigate and respond to complaints of fraud; (2) To Federal agencies, including but not limited to the Federal Trade Commission and DHS, involved in statistical analysis of data, and publication and/or reporting of aggregated or de-identified information designed to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of immigration proceedings at those agencies or to better meet EOIR's mission of adjudicating cases fairly, expeditiously, and uniformly interpreting and administering the Nation's immigration laws. (3) Where a record, either alone or in conjunction with other information, indicates a violation or potential violation of law-- criminal, civil, or regulatory in nature--to the appropriate federal, state, local, territorial, tribal, or foreign law enforcement authority or other appropriate entity charged with the responsibility for investigating or prosecuting such violation or charged with enforcing or implementing such law. (4) To any person or entity that EOIR has reason to believe possesses information regarding a matter within the jurisdiction of EOIR, to the extent deemed to be necessary by EOIR in order to elicit such information or cooperation from the recipient for use in the performance of an authorized activity, such as to assist EOIR in evaluating and substantiating claims of immigration-related fraud or abuse or unauthorized practice of immigration law. (5) In an appropriate proceeding before a court, grand jury, or administrative or adjudicative body, when the Department or an adjudicator determines that the records are relevant to the proceeding; or when the records are relevant to a proceeding and the DOJ or any subcomponent thereof is authorized to appear in it; or when any of the following is a party to litigation or has an interest in litigation: DOJ or any components thereof; any DOJ employee in his or her official capacity; any DOJ employee in his or her individual capacity where DOJ has agreed to represent the employee; or the United States where DOJ or any component thereof determines that litigation is likely to affect it or any of its subdivisions. (6) To an actual or potential party to litigation or the party's authorized representative for the purpose of negotiation or in connection with settlement, plea bargaining, or discovery proceedings, whether formal or informal. (7) To the news media and the public, including disclosures pursuant to 28 CFR 50.2, unless it is determined that release of the specific information in the context of a particular case would constitute an unwarranted invasion of personal privacy. (8) To contractors, grantees, experts, consultants, students, and others performing or working on a contract, service, grant, cooperative agreement, or other assignment for the agency, when necessary to accomplish an agency function related to this system of records. POLICIES AND PRACTICES FOR STORAGE OF RECORDS: All electronic records in this system are stored in electronic media via a configuration of government servers and in accordance with Federal and Department information security standards. Records received in hard [[Page 65605]] copy are digitized. Digitized copies undergo quality control checks to confirm the digitization of the records was complete and accurate before the original hard copy is disposed of in accordance with authorized agency record information management practices. Records in this system are maintained in accordance with applicable laws, rules, and policies on protecting individual privacy and operating in a Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program (FedRAMP) certified cloud-storage environment. Stored records are organized by complainant name, complaint or case number, complaint or case type and date, complaint or case status, and/or the name of the subject of the complaint or case. POLICIES AND PRACTICES FOR RETRIEVAL OF RECORDS: Records may be retrieved by complainant name, complaint or case type, complaint or case numbers, date the complaint or case was reported or closed, and/or the name of the subject of the complaint or case. Records may only be retrieved by authorized DOJ personnel on a need-to-know basis. POLICIES AND PRACTICES FOR RETENTION AND DISPOSAL OF RECORDS: Records are retained in accordance with EOIR Agency Records Control Schedule DAA-0582-2017-0001. The Immigration Fraud and Abuse Prevention Program Case Files and Data are temporary records retained for 15 years after cutoff (DAA-0582-2017-0001-0001 and DAA-0582-2017-0001-0002). The Fraud and Abuse Prevention Program Newsletters are temporary records disposed of 5 years after cutoff (DAA-0582-2017-0001-0003). The Fraud and Abuse Prevention Working Files are temporary records with a retention period of 3 years after cutoff (DAA-0582-2017-0001-0004). Fraud Program Items Closed without Action are temporary records retained for 3 years after cutoff (DAA-0582-2017-0001-0005). ADMINISTRATIVE, TECHNICAL, AND PHYSICAL SAFEGUARDS: Records in this system are safeguarded in accordance with applicable laws, rules, and policies that protect individual privacy, including automated system security and access policies of EOIR and the Department. Access to records is limited to Department personnel who have an official need for access in order to perform their duties. Electronic records are maintained and backed up on secure servers and networks that may only be accessed by authorized personnel. Physical records are stored in locked filing cabinets or locked offices until they are digitized and disposed of, as described above. Access to records may be restricted depending on a user's role and responsibility within the system. internet connections are protected by multiple firewalls. Security personnel conduct periodic vulnerability scans using DOJ-approved software to ensure security compliance, and security logs are enabled for all computers to assist in troubleshooting and forensics analysis during incident investigations. Users of individual computers can only gain access to the data by authorized user identification and authentication processes. Records are safeguarded in accordance with appropriate laws, rules, and policies based on the classification and handling restrictions of the record. If the A-number is not known, or the case occurred before 1988, the date of an Order to Show Cause, country of origin, and location of the immigration hearing must be provided.
